# Pharmaceutical Regulation

## Regulatory Authorities

### FDA (Food and Drug Administration) - USA
US federal agency responsible for approving drugs, medical devices, and biological products. Approval process: IND (Investigational New Drug) -> NDA (New Drug Application) or BLA (Biologics License Application). Standard review timeline: 10 months (Standard Review) or 6 months (Priority Review).

### FDA Accelerated Approval Pathways
- Fast Track: more frequent interactions with FDA during development
- Breakthrough Therapy: for therapies demonstrating substantial improvement over existing treatments
- Accelerated Approval: approval based on a surrogate endpoint, with mandatory confirmatory post-approval study
- Priority Review: review timeline reduced to 6 months
- RTOR (Real-Time Oncology Review): rolling review of submitted data for oncology treatments

### EMA (European Medicines Agency) - Europe
European agency based in Amsterdam. Grants centralized Marketing Authorization (MA) valid throughout the EU. Main committee: CHMP (Committee for Medicinal Products for Human Use). Review timeline: 210 active days. Procedures: centralized, decentralized, mutual recognition, national.

### Other Authorities
- PMDA (Japan): Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Agency
- NMPA (China): National Medical Products Administration, reformed in 2018 to accelerate approvals
- Health Canada: Canadian drug approval
- ANVISA (Brazil): National Health Surveillance Agency
- TGA (Australia): Therapeutic Goods Administration

## ICH (International Council for Harmonisation)

### Mission
Harmonize regulatory requirements between the US, Europe, and Japan to avoid study duplication. ICH guidelines are adopted by most global authorities.

### Key Guidelines
- ICH Q1-Q14: Quality (stability, validation, specifications, continuous manufacturing)
- ICH S1-S11: Safety (carcinogenicity, genotoxicity, reproductive toxicology)
- ICH E1-E20: Efficacy (clinical trial design, biostatistics, pharmacovigilance)
- ICH M1-M14: Multidisciplinary (MedDRA, CTD, eCTD)
- CTD (Common Technical Document): standardized format for registration dossier submission

## Pharmacovigilance

### Surveillance Systems
- FAERS (FDA Adverse Event Reporting System): US adverse event database
- EudraVigilance: European pharmacovigilance system
- PSUR/PBRER: periodic safety reports submitted regularly to authorities
- Signal detection: identification of new risks through statistical analysis of databases

### REMS (Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategies)
FDA program to manage specific risks of certain drugs. May include: medication guide, communication plan, elements to assure safe use (distribution restriction). Examples: isotretinoin (iPLEDGE), opioids, lenalidomide.

## Intellectual Property

### Types of Pharma Patents
- Composition of matter patent: protects the molecule itself (the strongest)
- Formulation patent: protects a specific formulation
- Method of use patent: protects a specific therapeutic indication
- Process patent: protects the manufacturing process

### Regulatory Exclusivities
- New Chemical Entity (NCE): 5 years of exclusivity in the US
- Biologic exclusivity: 12 years in the US (BPCIA), 8+2+1 years in Europe
- Orphan Drug exclusivity: 7 years in the US, 10 years in Europe
- Pediatric exclusivity: additional 6 months
- Patent Term Extension: compensation for time spent in regulatory review (max 5 years)

### Paragraph IV / ANDA
Process by which a generic manufacturer can challenge a patent by submitting an ANDA (Abbreviated New Drug Application) with a Paragraph IV certification asserting the patent is invalid or not infringed. Triggers litigation (Hatch-Waxman). The first generic to file receives 180 days of exclusivity.

## GMP (Good Manufacturing Practice)

### Principles
GMP ensures drugs are consistently manufactured according to quality standards. Covers: facilities, equipment, personnel, documentation, production, quality control, outsourcing, complaints, recalls.

### Inspections
FDA, EMA, and national authorities regularly inspect manufacturing sites. Types of observations: Form 483 (FDA), critical/major/minor deficiencies (EMA). An FDA Warning Letter can result in import bans. Inspections also cover CRO sites and clinical trial centers.

## Data Integrity
Regulatory authorities strongly emphasize data integrity (ALCOA+: Attributable, Legible, Contemporaneous, Original, Accurate + Complete, Consistent, Enduring, Available). Data integrity violations result in severe consequences including Warning Letters and import bans.
