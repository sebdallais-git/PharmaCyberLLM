# Top 20 Pharmaceutical Companies by Revenue (FY2024)

Source: Annual reports and SEC filings. All figures in USD. For non-US companies, converted at average 2024 exchange rates. Revenue figures are total company revenue (pharma segments noted where applicable).

## Rankings

### 1. Johnson & Johnson (USA)
FY2024 Revenue: ~$88.0B. Post-Kenvue consumer health spin-off (2023), J&J now focuses on Innovative Medicine (~$57B) and MedTech (~$31B). Key drugs: Darzalex ($11.7B, multiple myeloma), Stelara ($10B, immunology, biosimilar entry 2025), Tremfya (IL-23 inhibitor), Erleada (prostate cancer). Headquarters: New Brunswick, NJ.

### 2. Roche (Switzerland)
FY2024 Revenue: ~$66B (CHF 58.9B). Divided between Pharmaceuticals (~$44B) and Diagnostics (~$22B). Key drugs: Ocrevus ($7.5B, multiple sclerosis), Hemlibra ($5B, hemophilia A), Tecentriq (oncology), Vabysmo (ophthalmology, rapid growth). Strong diagnostics integration as competitive advantage.

### 3. Merck & Co (USA)
FY2024 Revenue: ~$64B. Dominated by Keytruda (~$27B, immuno-oncology blockbuster, patent cliff ~2028). Other key drugs: Gardasil ($8.9B, HPV vaccine), Januvia/Janumet (diabetes, facing IRA price negotiation), Lynparza (oncology), Lagevrio. Major pipeline investment to offset Keytruda patent cliff. Known as MSD outside North America.

### 4. Pfizer (USA)
FY2024 Revenue: ~$59B. Revenue normalized after COVID peak of $100B+ in 2022. Key drugs: Eliquis ($6.7B, shared with BMS, patent 2026), Prevnar family ($6.4B, pneumococcal vaccines), Ibrance, Xtandi, Padcev (acquired via Seagen $43B deal). Seagen acquisition brings ADC oncology pipeline.

### 5. AbbVie (USA)
FY2024 Revenue: ~$56B. Managing post-Humira patent cliff (biosimilars launched US 2023, from peak $21B). Growth drivers: Skyrizi ($10B+, IL-23, psoriasis/Crohn's) and Rinvoq ($5.5B+, JAK inhibitor). Also strong in aesthetics (Botox via Allergan acquisition) and neuroscience. Diversification strategy proving effective.

### 6. AstraZeneca (UK/Sweden)
FY2024 Revenue: ~$54B. Fastest-growing big pharma. Oncology powerhouse: Tagrisso ($5.7B, lung cancer), Imfinzi ($4.7B, immuno-oncology), Enhertu (ADC, partnered with Daiichi Sankyo, rapid growth). Also strong in rare diseases (Alexion portfolio: Soliris/Ultomiris). Target: $80B revenue by 2030.

### 7. Novartis (Switzerland)
FY2024 Revenue: ~$50B (post-Sandoz generics spin-off 2023). Focused pure-play pharma. Key drugs: Entresto ($7.5B, heart failure), Cosentyx ($5B, immunology), Kisqali (breast cancer, potential blockbuster), Pluvicto (radioligand therapy). Strong pipeline in cardiovascular, immunology, neuroscience.

### 8. Sanofi (France)
FY2024 Revenue: ~$46B. Key drugs: Dupixent ($13B+, blockbuster in atopic dermatitis/asthma, partnered with Regeneron), vaccines division (Fluzone, Beyfortus RSV), rare diseases (Cerezyme). Planning consumer health separation. Heavy investment in immunology pipeline and mRNA platform.

### 9. Eli Lilly (USA)
FY2024 Revenue: ~$45B. Extraordinary growth driven by GLP-1 franchise: Mounjaro/Zepbound (tirzepatide, diabetes/obesity, combined $15B+), Verzenio ($4.8B, breast cancer), Taltz, Jardiance. Demand for obesity drugs exceeding supply capacity. Investing $20B+ in manufacturing expansion.

### 10. Bristol-Myers Squibb (USA)
FY2024 Revenue: ~$44B. Facing significant patent cliff: Eliquis (shared with Pfizer, patent 2026), Opdivo ($9B, immuno-oncology), Revlimid ($5B, declining). New growth assets: Reblozyl (anemia), Camzyos (cardiac), Opdualag. Acquired Karuna ($14B) for schizophrenia asset KarXT and RayzeBio for radiopharmaceuticals.

### 11. Novo Nordisk (Denmark)
FY2024 Revenue: ~$42B (DKK 290B). Dominating the GLP-1/obesity market alongside Eli Lilly. Key drugs: Ozempic ($18B+, type 2 diabetes), Wegovy ($8B+, obesity), Rybelsus (oral GLP-1), insulin portfolio (Tresiba, Levemir). Also strong in rare diseases (hemophilia). World's most valuable pharma by market cap.

### 12. GSK (UK)
FY2024 Revenue: ~$38B. Post-Haleon consumer health spin-off, focused on biopharma and vaccines. Key drugs: Shingrix ($4B, shingles vaccine), Dovato/Tivicay (HIV), Nucala (asthma), Arexvy (RSV vaccine for older adults). Growing oncology pipeline via Tesaro acquisition (Zejula).

### 13. Amgen (USA)
FY2024 Revenue: ~$33B. Large-cap biotech. Key drugs: Enbrel ($3.5B, declining), Prolia/XGEVA ($4.5B, bone health), Repatha (PCSK9 cholesterol), Lumakras (KRAS oncology), Tezspire (asthma). Acquired Horizon Therapeutics ($27.8B) adding rare disease portfolio (Tepezza, Krystexxa). Obesity pipeline with MariTide (oral GLP-1 competitor).

### 14. Takeda (Japan)
FY2024 Revenue: ~$31B (¥4,300B). Japan's largest pharma. Key drugs: Entyvio ($7B, GI inflammation), immunoglobulin therapies, Ninlaro (oncology), rare disease portfolio. Strong plasma-derived therapies business. Global diversification with 80% of revenue from outside Japan.

### 15. Gilead Sciences (USA)
FY2024 Revenue: ~$28B. HIV franchise dominates: Biktarvy ($13B+, largest HIV drug globally), Descovy, Truvada. Oncology growth via cell therapies (Yescarta, Tecartus). Lenacapavir (twice-yearly HIV prevention injection) represents major pipeline opportunity. Hepatitis C portfolio declining.

### 16. Boehringer Ingelheim (Germany)
FY2024 Revenue: ~$27B. Largest privately held pharma company. Key drugs: Jardiance ($7B+, diabetes/heart failure, partnered with Lilly), Ofev (IPF), Spiriva (COPD, declining). Strong animal health division. Significant investment in oncology and immunology pipeline. Private status allows long-term R&D investment.

### 17. Bayer Pharma Division (Germany)
FY2024 Pharma Revenue: ~$19B (total Bayer ~$50B including Crop Science and Consumer Health). Key drugs: Eylea ($4B+, ophthalmology, declining due to biosimilars), Xarelto ($3B, declining due to generic entry), Nubeqa (prostate cancer, growing), Kerendia (CKD). Pharma division under pressure; company considering structural options.

### 18. Teva Pharmaceutical (Israel)
FY2024 Revenue: ~$16.5B. World's largest generic drug manufacturer. Key branded product: Austedo ($1.7B, tardive dyskinesia/Huntington's chorea). Generics portfolio spans 3,500+ products in 60 countries. Biosimilar portfolio growing (Ajovy migraine). Navigating opioid litigation settlements ($4.35B). Turnaround strategy under CEO Richard Francis.

### 19. Regeneron (USA)
FY2024 Revenue: ~$14.5B. Biotech powerhouse. Key drugs: Eylea/Eylea HD ($6B+, wet AMD), Dupixent royalties from Sanofi partnership ($4B+ in royalties/collaboration revenue), Libtayo (immuno-oncology). Strong R&D productivity with VelociSuite antibody discovery platform. Pipeline includes obesity (trevogrumab), oncology, inflammation.

### 20. Biogen (USA)
FY2024 Revenue: ~$9.5B. Focus on neuroscience. Key drugs: multiple sclerosis portfolio (Tysabri, Tecfidera declining, Vumerity), Spinraza ($1.5B, spinal muscular atrophy), Leqembi (Alzheimer's, partnered with Eisai, slow launch). Acquired Reata Pharmaceuticals ($7.3B) adding Skyclarys (Friedreich's ataxia). Biosimilar portfolio contributing revenue.

## Key Trends in Pharma Revenue (2024-2025)

### GLP-1 Revolution
The GLP-1 receptor agonist class (Novo Nordisk's Ozempic/Wegovy and Eli Lilly's Mounjaro/Zepbound) is reshaping revenue rankings. Combined class revenue exceeded $50B in 2024, with projections of $100B+ by 2030. This is driving Eli Lilly and Novo Nordisk up the revenue rankings rapidly.

### Patent Cliff Impact
The 2025-2030 patent cliff threatens ~$200B in branded drug revenue industry-wide. Most affected: Merck (Keytruda), BMS (Eliquis, Opdivo), Pfizer (Eliquis), AbbVie (Humira already declining), J&J (Stelara).

### Oncology Dominance
Oncology remains the largest therapeutic area by revenue (~$220B globally), driven by immuno-oncology, ADCs (antibody-drug conjugates), bispecific antibodies, cell therapies, and radioligand therapies.

### Revenue Concentration Risk
Many companies depend heavily on 1-2 blockbusters: Merck on Keytruda (42% of revenue), Novo Nordisk on Ozempic/Wegovy (62%), Gilead on Biktarvy (46%). This creates significant downside risk at patent expiration.
