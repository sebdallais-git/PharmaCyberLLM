# Top 20 Pharmaceutical Companies by Reputation (2024-2025)

Sources: RepTrak Global Pharma Reputation Report (2024), PatientView Corporate Reputation Survey, Fortune World's Most Admired Companies (2024-2025), Axios-Harris Poll 100, Glassdoor Best Places to Work, S&P Global ESG scores, and industry analyst assessments.

Reputation in pharma is multi-dimensional: public trust, patient group perception, investor confidence, employee satisfaction, and ESG performance. Rankings below synthesize multiple sources. Scores are composite estimates on a 0-100 scale.

## Rankings

### 1. Novo Nordisk — Score: 82/100
Consistently ranked as the most reputable pharma company globally. Strong corporate purpose ("driving change to defeat diabetes and other serious chronic diseases"). Industry-leading ESG performance: carbon-neutral operations since 2020, RE100 member, zero-waste manufacturing targets for 2030. Patient access programs distribute insulin at or below $3/vial in low-income countries. Foundation-owned governance structure (Novo Nordisk Foundation) ensures long-term perspective. Glassdoor rating: 4.2/5. Concerns: Ozempic/Wegovy pricing criticism in the US, supply shortages.

### 2. Roche — Score: 80/100
Top reputation for scientific innovation and diagnostics-pharma integration. Pioneer in personalized healthcare (companion diagnostics for targeted therapies). Strong patient access programs: Roche Patient Solutions covers 4.5M+ patients in low-income countries. Recognized for transparency in clinical trial data sharing (clinicalstudydatarequest.com). Glassdoor rating: 4.1/5. Family-controlled governance provides stability. Criticism: drug pricing in the US, slow response to biosimilar competition.

### 3. Johnson & Johnson — Score: 77/100
Historically #1 in reputation (trusted consumer brand since 1886). J&J Credo (corporate philosophy since 1943) defines responsibilities to patients, employees, communities, and shareholders. Major philanthropic programs. However, reputation significantly damaged by talc/asbestos litigation (multi-billion dollar settlements), opioid crisis liability, and controversial use of subsidiary bankruptcy ("Texas two-step"). COVID-19 vaccine contribution provided temporary reputation boost. Glassdoor rating: 4.0/5.

### 4. AstraZeneca — Score: 76/100
Reputation transformed post-COVID-19. Provided COVID vaccine at cost to low-income countries (3B+ doses delivered). Strong science-driven reputation under CEO Pascal Soriot (since 2012). Named among Fortune's Most Admired Companies. Strong ESG commitments: Ambition Zero Carbon by 2045. Access to Medicine Foundation top performer. Glassdoor rating: 4.1/5. Criticism: vaccine blood clot controversy in 2021 impacted European public perception.

### 5. Novartis — Score: 75/100
Reputation rebuilt after past compliance issues (kickback settlements). Strong focus on access to medicines: Novartis Global Health delivers treatments to 77 countries at reduced or zero cost. Sandoz spin-off simplified the corporate narrative. AI-forward R&D strategy positions company as innovator. Glassdoor rating: 4.0/5. Concerns: Zolgensma pricing controversy ($2.1M per treatment), historical bribery/corruption cases.

### 6. Eli Lilly — Score: 74/100
Growing reputation alongside market performance. Named among Fortune's Most Admired Companies (healthcare category leader). Strong employee satisfaction (Glassdoor 4.1/5, "Great Place to Work" certified). $30 insulin cap commitment improved public image. Significant community investment in Indianapolis. Lilly 30x30 initiative targets 30M people in resource-limited settings by 2030. Concerns: Mounjaro/Zepbound pricing and access inequities, insulin pricing history.

### 7. Merck & Co — Score: 73/100
Respected for scientific heritage (Merck Manual, vaccine innovation legacy). Keytruda success demonstrates R&D prowess. Strong Access to Medicine Foundation ranking. Merck for Mothers program (global maternal mortality reduction) widely praised. MECTIZAN Donation Program (river blindness, 30+ years) is a landmark pharma philanthropy effort. Glassdoor rating: 4.0/5. Concerns: pricing criticism, workforce restructuring.

### 8. GSK — Score: 72/100
Reputation improved post-Haleon spin-off with renewed focus on vaccines and specialty medicines. Strong vaccine access programs (Gavi partnership). CEO Emma Walmsley credited with cultural transformation and R&D refocus. Named in Dow Jones Sustainability Index. Glassdoor rating: 3.8/5. Historical issues: China bribery scandal (2014), Zantac litigation, consumer health controversies.

### 9. Sanofi — Score: 71/100
Strong reputation in vaccines (global leader in flu vaccines) and rare diseases. Sanofi Global Health Unit delivers essential medicines at not-for-profit prices in 40 lowest-income countries. Dupixent has built strong patient community engagement. Foundation S partnership model for global health. Glassdoor rating: 3.9/5. Concerns: restructuring/layoff announcements, French labor relations tensions.

### 10. Amgen — Score: 70/100
Respected as pioneering biotech company (founded 1980). Strong employee culture (regularly in Fortune 100 Best Companies to Work For). Amgen Foundation has invested $400M+ in science education. Safety Together (biosimilar access programs). Glassdoor rating: 4.1/5. Concerns: Enbrel pricing criticism, aggressive legal strategies against biosimilar competitors, Horizon Therapeutics acquisition scrutiny.

### 11. Vertex Pharmaceuticals — Score: 70/100
Exceptional reputation in the cystic fibrosis community for transforming patient outcomes (Trikafta). Named #1 biopharma in Science magazine's Top Employers. Strong employee engagement (Glassdoor 4.0/5). Patient access programs cover 100% of eligible uninsured US patients. Pioneering gene editing (Casgevy approval with CRISPR Therapeutics). Concerns: CF drug pricing ($322K/year for Trikafta), limited therapeutic diversity until recently.

### 12. Gilead Sciences — Score: 69/100
Reputation shaped by two narratives: hepatitis C cure (Sovaldi/Harvoni, medical breakthrough) and HIV treatment leadership (Biktarvy). Lenacapavir for HIV prevention seen as game-changing. Strong LGBTQ+ community engagement. Criticized for: Sovaldi launch pricing ($84K), slow response on PrEP affordability, Remdesivir COVID pricing debates. Glassdoor rating: 3.9/5.

### 13. AbbVie — Score: 68/100
Mixed reputation. Respected for R&D productivity and successful Humira lifecycle management. Strong employee programs (Great Place to Work certified). AbbVie Foundation focuses on STEM education. However, heavily criticized for: Humira patent thicket strategy (130+ patents blocking biosimilars), aggressive pricing practices, political lobbying on drug pricing legislation. Glassdoor rating: 4.1/5.

### 14. Takeda — Score: 68/100
Respected for 240+ year history (founded 1781, oldest pharma company). Strong reputation in Japan, building global presence. Takeda values-based management (Takeda-ism: Integrity, Fairness, Honesty, Perseverance). Access to Medicine Foundation top 10. Patient assistance programs in 70+ countries. Glassdoor rating: 3.8/5. Concerns: post-Shire integration complexity, Japanese corporate culture adaptation.

### 15. Boehringer Ingelheim — Score: 68/100
Strong reputation as the largest privately held pharma company. Private ownership enables patient-first messaging without quarterly earnings pressure. "Value through Innovation" philosophy. Strong animal health reputation. Making More Health social entrepreneurship program active in 60+ countries. Known for long-term employee tenure and R&D commitment. Less public scrutiny due to private status. Family-owned since 1885.

### 16. Bristol-Myers Squibb — Score: 66/100
Historic reputation for oncology innovation (first immuno-oncology checkpoint inhibitor Yervoy). BMS Foundation (community health, health equity). Strong diversity and inclusion programs. Concerns: Celgene acquisition integration issues, patent cliff management, aggressive patent strategies on Revlimid, workforce reductions affecting morale. Glassdoor rating: 3.8/5.

### 17. Pfizer — Score: 65/100
Reputation polarized post-COVID. COVID vaccine development (100 days from sequence to trial) was a remarkable achievement, but subsequent public backlash from anti-vaccine movements. Pre-COVID reputation mixed: respected for R&D scale but criticized for aggressive pricing and M&A-driven strategy. Post-COVID revenue decline and Paxlovid write-downs impacted investor confidence. Glassdoor rating: 3.9/5.

### 18. Bayer — Score: 62/100
Reputation severely damaged by Monsanto acquisition (2018, $63B). Roundup/glyphosate litigation resulted in $10B+ in settlements and ongoing legal risk. Environmental and GMO concerns. Pharma division reputation relatively intact but overshadowed by crop science controversies. Historic German pharmaceutical heritage (aspirin inventor, founded 1863). ESG rating downgraded. Glassdoor rating: 3.7/5.

### 19. Teva Pharmaceutical — Score: 58/100
Reputation damaged by opioid crisis involvement ($4.35B settlement), price-fixing allegations in generic drugs, and accounting controversies. Highest generic drug market share globally but perceived as commoditized. Turnaround under CEO Richard Francis showing progress. Austedo (branded drug) and biosimilar pipeline rebuilding portfolio reputation. Glassdoor rating: 3.5/5. Cost-cutting and layoffs have impacted employee morale.

### 20. Biogen — Score: 57/100
Reputation damaged by Aduhelm (aducanumab) controversy: FDA accelerated approval despite negative advisory committee vote, CMS coverage restrictions, $56K annual price, and ultimately withdrawn from market. Leqembi (lecanemab, partnered with Eisai) represents a reset. Historic contributions to MS treatment respected. Workforce reductions and strategic uncertainty affecting reputation. Glassdoor rating: 3.4/5.

## Reputation Measurement Methodology

### Key Dimensions Assessed
1. **Products & Services**: Drug efficacy, safety, patient outcomes, innovation
2. **Governance & Ethics**: Compliance record, transparency, lobbying practices, executive compensation
3. **Citizenship & ESG**: Environmental sustainability, access to medicine programs, community investment
4. **Workplace**: Employee satisfaction, diversity & inclusion, talent retention
5. **Financial Performance**: Consistent growth, shareholder returns, fiscal responsibility
6. **Leadership & Vision**: CEO reputation, strategic clarity, communication

### Major Reputation Benchmarks Used
- **RepTrak**: Surveys 80,000+ individuals across 15 countries on familiarity and perception of pharma companies. Measures esteem, trust, admiration, and good feeling.
- **PatientView**: Surveys 1,500+ patient organizations globally on pharma corporate reputation. Focus on patient-centricity, transparency, quality of information provided.
- **Access to Medicine Foundation Index**: Ranks top 20 pharma companies on efforts to improve access to medicines in 106 low- and middle-income countries. Published biennially.
- **Fortune Most Admired Companies**: Surveys executives, directors, and analysts on quality of management, innovation, social responsibility, and long-term investment value.
- **Axios-Harris Poll 100**: US-focused survey of 16,310 Americans on corporate reputation covering ethics, trajectory, culture, trust, and citizenship.

## Industry-Wide Reputation Challenges

### Structural Perception Issues
The pharmaceutical industry consistently ranks among the least trusted industries in public surveys (typically bottom 3 alongside tobacco and oil/gas in Gallup polls). Key drivers: drug pricing opacity, opioid crisis legacy, perceived profiteering, political lobbying against price reforms.

### COVID-19 Double-Edged Sword
COVID vaccine development briefly improved pharma's public image (Gallup favorability rose from 27% to 40% in 2020-2021), but gains reversed as vaccine hesitancy and conspiracy theories grew. Post-pandemic, public favorability returned to ~30%.

### Drug Pricing as Dominant Concern
In US polling, 82% of adults say prescription drug costs are unreasonable (KFF Health Tracking Poll 2024). The Inflation Reduction Act (IRA) Medicare negotiation represents first US government drug price negotiation, partially addressing public concern.

### ESG and Sustainability
Increasing investor focus on ESG performance in pharma. Key issues: carbon emissions from manufacturing, water usage, supply chain ethics, clinical trial diversity, antibiotic resistance stewardship, access to medicines in developing countries.
