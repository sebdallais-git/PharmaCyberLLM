# Pharmaceutical Industry Fundamentals

## Drug Development Pipeline

Drug development follows strict regulatory phases:

### Preclinical Phase
Laboratory research and animal model testing to evaluate initial safety and efficacy of a drug candidate. This phase lasts on average 3 to 6 years. Studies include pharmacokinetics (ADME: Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, Excretion), toxicology, and pharmacodynamics.

### Phase I - Safety
First-in-human trials, typically on 20 to 100 healthy volunteers. Objective: evaluate tolerability, pharmacokinetics, and determine the Maximum Tolerated Dose (MTD). Average duration: 1 year.

### Phase II - Efficacy
Trials on 100 to 300 patients with the target disease. Objective: confirm therapeutic efficacy and refine dosing. Studies are generally randomized and controlled. Duration: 1 to 2 years.

### Phase III - Confirmation
Large-scale trials on 1,000 to 5,000 patients. Objective: confirm efficacy, monitor side effects, and compare to standard of care. These pivotal studies are required for registration. Duration: 2 to 4 years.

### Registration and Approval
Dossier submission to authorities: FDA (United States), EMA (Europe), PMDA (Japan). The NDA/BLA (FDA) or MAA (EMA) compiles all preclinical and clinical data. Review timeline: 6 to 18 months.

### Phase IV - Pharmacovigilance
Post-marketing surveillance to detect rare adverse effects and confirm the benefit/risk profile in real-world conditions.

## Drug Types

### Small Molecules
Traditional chemical drugs, often administered orally. Examples: aspirin, statins, kinase inhibitors. Manufactured through chemical synthesis.

### Biologics
Therapeutic proteins produced by biological systems. Include monoclonal antibodies (mAb), fusion proteins, growth factors. Examples: adalimumab (Humira), trastuzumab (Herceptin).

### Gene and Cell Therapies
Innovative treatments that modify genetic material or use engineered cells. Include CAR-T cells (Kymriah, Yescarta), mRNA-based therapies, and AAV viral vectors.

### Biosimilars
Copies of off-patent biologics. Must demonstrate similarity in quality, efficacy, and safety. Rapidly growing market.

## Major Industry Players

### Big Pharma
Largest pharmaceutical companies by revenue: Pfizer, Johnson & Johnson, Roche, Novartis, Merck & Co, AbbVie, Sanofi, AstraZeneca, GSK, Eli Lilly, Bristol-Myers Squibb, Novo Nordisk.

### Biotech
Companies specializing in biotechnology: Amgen, Gilead Sciences, Regeneron, Moderna, BioNTech, Vertex Pharmaceuticals, CRISPR Therapeutics.

### CRO (Contract Research Organizations)
Organizations that conduct clinical trials: IQVIA, Covance (LabCorp), PPD, Parexel, Syneos Health.

### CDMO (Contract Development and Manufacturing Organizations)
Contract manufacturers: Lonza, Samsung Biologics, Catalent, WuXi Biologics, Thermo Fisher Scientific.

## Current Trends

### AI in Pharma
AI is used for target discovery, virtual screening, lead optimization, toxicity prediction, and clinical trial data analysis. Key companies: Insilico Medicine, Recursion, Exscientia.

### mRNA Therapies
Following the success of Pfizer/BioNTech and Moderna COVID-19 vaccines, mRNA technology is being explored for other vaccines (flu, RSV, cancer) and therapies.

### GLP-1 Drugs for Obesity
GLP-1 receptor agonists (semaglutide/Wegovy, tirzepatide/Mounjaro) represent an exploding market for obesity and type 2 diabetes treatment. Novo Nordisk and Eli Lilly dominate this segment.

### Precision Medicine
Treatments adapted to the patient's genetic profile. Biomarkers and companion diagnostics allow selection of patients most likely to respond to treatment.

### Cell and Gene Therapies
CAR-T cells, in vivo gene therapies (Luxturna, Zolgensma), and genome editing (CRISPR) are transforming treatment of rare diseases and certain cancers.
